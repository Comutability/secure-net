## 1. Problem

Secure Net had the pieces of private communication (signed records, sessions, federation between
sites) but no open social protocol on top: no shared idea of an actor, a post, a follow, or how
they travel between sites. The two open social protocols in wide use each solve half of what is
needed, and neither is private:

- **ActivityPub** has a well understood vocabulary and server-to-server delivery, but identity
  belongs to the server, moving is partial, and servers read everything, including followers-only
  posts and direct messages.
- **The AT Protocol** has portable identity, signed repositories and extensible schemas, but its
  repositories are public and private data is not yet part of it.

Neither uses post-quantum cryptography.

## 2. Proposed change

Five new parts, built on CVP-002 and CVP-016:

- **CVP-017 Actors and discovery.** An actor is a quantum-resistant key; its id is that key's id.
  A signed actor document carries its handle (`name@host`), profile and devices. Sites serve actor
  documents and prekeys but can't forge them. Actors can move between sites and keep their id,
  keys, followers and sessions.
- **CVP-018 Activities.** ActivityStreams names (`Create`, `Note`, `Follow`, `Like`, `Announce`...)
  as plain canonical JSON, each activity signed on the device that made it with Ed25519 and
  ML-DSA-65.
- **CVP-019 Delivery.** Three envelope modes: `public` (signed, readable), `direct` (a CVP-016
  session per device) and `audience` (one CVP-016 sender-key message for all followers or group
  members, rekeyed when anyone leaves). Sites run outboxes, mailboxes and site-to-site delivery,
  and can read only public envelopes.
- **CVP-020 Repositories.** An actor's public activities as a Merkle-rooted set with signed
  commits: verifiable from any copy, portable, with a change feed for mirrors and indexers.
- **CVP-021 Lexicons.** New object types named by reverse domain names, with schemas signed by that
  domain's `lexicons` actor; unknown types degrade to ordinary posts.

Plus registry entries (record kinds, features, labels, endpoints, vocabulary), a `social`
conformance profile, vectors for all five parts, a reference site and client, tests and an
example. Secure Net 0.5.0.

## 3. Benefits and intended uses

Commonveil and anything else built on Secure Net can offer what people expect from the fediverse
(handles, following, public posts, replies, likes, groups) and from the AT Protocol (taking your
account with you, public data anyone can verify and index, app-defined record types), while
followers-only posts, groups, direct messages and the social graph itself stay unreadable to every
site. Familiar names make it approachable to developers who know either protocol.

## 4. Security and privacy

Each part has its own section; the overall table of what each party learns is in CVP-019. In
short: content, activity types, followers lists and group membership are end to end encrypted with
X25519 and ML-KEM-768 together and signed with Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65. Sites still see who delivers
to whom, when, and how much, and the sender of a session's first messages.

This is security-critical design and code written by an AI and it needs qualified human review
before it moves past Technical Review. It inherits CVP-016, which is itself unreviewed. Points
that most need a reviewer:

- binding of activities and sender keys to the device a message came from (CVP-019, 1.1);
- audience rekeying and its cost for large audiences;
- the trust model for handles, `moved` answers and first contact (CVP-017);
- that signed activities are non-repudiable, including in direct messages (CVP-018);
- backdating by a compromised device before its removal (CVP-017);
- spam and abuse handling when sites can't read what they carry.

## 5. Compatibility

Additive. New features `cvp.actors`, `cvp.activities`, `cvp.delivery`, `cvp.repos` and
`cvp.lexicons`; new endpoints under `/cvp/v2/`. Sites that don't announce them are unaffected.
It is **not** wire-compatible with ActivityPub or the AT Protocol, by design: both let servers
read what they carry. Public activities map one to one onto ActivityStreams, so a bridge for
public posts is possible later (CVP-009).

## 6. Effects on independent implementations

A client or site needs CVP-002 and CVP-016 first. The vectors give fixed keys, an actor document,
valid and invalid activities and envelopes, Merkle roots and proofs, a repository and a lexicon.
The reference site (`src/social/site.js`) is about 250 lines and shows everything a server must do.

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **Implement ActivityPub itself, with encrypted payloads.** Other servers would still see
  addressing and activity types, would not understand the payloads, and identity would stay with
  the server.
- **Implement the AT Protocol itself.** Its repositories are public by design; adding private
  data would mean a parallel protocol anyway, and its signatures are classical.
- **MLS for audiences.** Better for large groups, but there is no standard post-quantum MLS
  ciphersuite yet (as in SNIP-0003). Sender keys can be replaced later behind the same envelope.
- **JSON-LD.** Rejected: more than one encoding of the same thing can't be signed safely, and
  remote contexts are a privacy and availability problem.

## 8. Testing and migration

`test/social.test.mjs` (11 tests; 22 security checks were removed one at a time, and each
removal either failed a test or was a duplicate of a check that did): handles; self-certifying actor documents and rollback; device
history; vocabulary rules; envelope rules; audiences and removal; forged, altered and replayed
site deliveries; an end-to-end run through two sites; repositories with tampered copies and
inclusion proofs; lexicons and authority; moving between sites. `examples/social.mjs` runs three
sites and four people through a network that records everything and checks that no private text
or activity type appears in the record. Nothing existing needs migrating; adopting this in
Commonveil is a separate change.

## 9. Implementation evidence

`src/social/` in Secure Net 0.5.0. The Merkle roots in the CVP-020 vectors were reproduced by a
separate implementation in Python. The standards checker (SNIP-0004) verifies that every new
name is registered.
