## Problem

Requests between sites were protected only by TLS. Anything in between, or anyone recording
traffic to decrypt once classical key exchange is broken, could learn who writes to whom, in
which conversation, and when. Site and record signatures were Ed25519 alone.

## Proposal

As CVP-015: seal request bodies site to site with ephemeral P-256 ECDH and ML-KEM-768, HKDF-SHA-256
and AES-256-GCM, bound to the two sites and the endpoint; seal replies under a one-time key
carried in the request; sign requests with Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65; pin relay keys with the site
key and refuse downgrades. Allow signed records to use Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65 keys.

## Security

Open questions a reviewer should examine:

- the hybrid combiner (concatenated shared secrets through HKDF, with the ephemeral key and KEM
  ciphertext bound by hash) against the published guidance for hybrid KEMs;
- that both signatures are required by the verifier's pinned view of the sender, never by what
  the request claims;
- first contact is still trust-on-first-use;
- device signatures on messages and VOPRF tokens remain classical.

## Compatibility

Additive. A site starts sealing when it updates; others keep working with it unsealed until
they update, and can't be talked back down once they have.
