## 1. Problem

GOVERNANCE.md gives the Technical Steering Committee (TSC) the decision on every SNIP, and a
future Community Council the appeals. It deliberately leaves out how either body is formed,
how many members must agree, how long they serve, and how they are held to account. Until those
are written down:

- every decision rests on one person, the founding steward, without a stated end to that;
- nobody outside can tell how to join, object or appeal in practice;
- the protocol can't credibly claim to be independent of Comutability's own products.

This proposal is that policy. It sits under the Founding Charter v1.1, ORG-001 and GOV-002, and
gives way to them where they disagree.

## 2. Proposed change

### 2.1 Who does what

| | Technical Steering Committee | Community Council |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decides | Whether a SNIP is accepted or rejected; part statuses; releases of the standard | Appeals against TSC decisions; changes to GOVERNANCE.md and this policy; confirming TSC appointments; recalls |
| Does not | Change governance alone | Write or rewrite specifications |
| Size | 3 to 5 | 5 to 7 |
| Chosen for | Technical contributions, competence, accountability | Representing contributors, implementers, site operators and users |

### 2.2 The TSC

- **Seats.** Once it has three members: at least one is not employed by, paid by or contracting
  for Comutability or a product built by its teams; at least one is a qualified security reviewer,
  or the TSC keeps a published list of named external security reviewers it uses; no more than
  one third of members share an employer.
- **Appointment.** A candidate is nominated publicly (any contributor may nominate, including
  themselves). The nomination is open for 14 days of comment. The TSC then decides, and the
  Community Council confirms once it exists.
- **Terms.** Two years, renewable, staggered so that no more than half the seats end in the same
  year. A member may step down at any time.
- **Quorum and thresholds.**
  - Quorum: a majority of members.
  - To accept an additive change: a majority of members voting.
  - To accept a breaking change, or to mark a part Stable: two thirds of members, and no
    unresolved objection from the security reviewer for a security-sensitive change.
  - To reject: a majority of members voting, with written reasons.
- **Conflicts of interest.** A member with a material interest in a proposal says so in the
  record and does not vote on it. Being its author counts.
- **AI.** As GOVERNANCE.md says: AI may prepare proposals, code, tests and reviews, but can't be a
  member, can't vote, and its approval never stands in for a human's.

### 2.3 The founder phase

Until the TSC has three members including one independent member:

- The founding steward is the provisional TSC. Every decision record says "decided by the
  single-member provisional TSC".
- A part may be marked Accepted only after a named, qualified human security reviewer other than
  the founding steward has reviewed any security-sensitive content.
- No part may be marked Stable.
- Every Accepted decision is provisional. The first full TSC reconfirms or reopens each one within
  six months of forming.
- Appeals go to the founding steward, who answers in writing within 14 days. The answer says the
  Community Council doesn't exist yet and that the appeal may be renewed once it does.
- The founding steward publishes, every three months, how many independent contributors there are
  and what is being done to recruit members.

### 2.4 The Community Council

- **When it forms.** Once at least 15 people outside Comutability's staff have had contributions
  merged in the last 12 months, or at least three sites run by other people federate with The
  Agora. Whichever comes first triggers the first election, which must start within three months.
- **Seats.** Contributors elect three; site operators and independent implementers elect two;
  one seat is held by a founding steward until the second election and then becomes a seat
  elected by contributors.
- **Electorate.**
  - Contributors: anyone with a contribution merged in any Comutability repository in the last 12
    months, including documentation, translation and reviews.
  - Operators: one vote per site that publicly federates.
- **How elections are run.** Elections may use the protocol's own voting (CVP-012) on The Agora,
  but until CVP-012 is Accepted the official count is the one published on GitHub, with the
  method and the anonymised tally.
- **Terms.** Two years, staggered.

### 2.5 Appeals

1. Anyone may appeal a TSC decision within 30 days, by opening an issue on the proposal that says
   what is wrong: the process wasn't followed, a comment went unanswered, or the decision causes a
   harm (security, privacy, accessibility) the TSC didn't weigh.
2. The TSC answers in writing within 14 days.
3. If the appellant isn't satisfied, the Community Council decides within 30 days. It may uphold
   the decision, or send it back to the TSC with written reasons. It can't rewrite the
   specification.
4. A decision sent back twice on the same grounds is reconsidered at a joint meeting of both
   bodies, and the outcome needs two thirds of the combined members present.

### 2.6 Recalls

Ten contributors, or a third of the other members of the body, can propose removing a member for
persistent absence, an undeclared conflict, or conduct against the code of conduct. The body that
appointed or elected the member decides, after hearing them, by two thirds.

### 2.7 Records

- Every decision is written into its SNIP's section 10, with who took part, how they voted,
  declared conflicts and reasons.
- A decision register (`DECISIONS.md` in this repository) lists every decision of either body, in
  order, with links. comutability.org shows it.
- Meetings are minuted publicly. A session is private only for an embargoed security issue or a
  personal matter, and its minutes then say what was discussed in general terms and when the rest
  will be published.

## 3. Benefits and intended uses

It turns "independent" in GOVERNANCE.md into something checkable. It gives implementers and
contributors a known route in, a known route to object, and a known end to the founder phase.

## 4. Security and privacy

Security-sensitive changes keep their mandatory human security review, which is strengthened
during the founder phase. Votes and elections publish anonymised tallies only.

## 5. Compatibility

None: no wire format, label or rule changes.

## 6. Effects on independent implementations

They gain guaranteed seats on the TSC once it forms, a vote for the Community Council as
operators or implementers, and a right of appeal. Nothing here favours Comutability's own
applications: their teams propose and appeal like anyone else.

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **One Standards Council** combining technical and community seats. Simpler, but mixes technical
  review with representation and leaves no separate appeal.
- **The Community Council decides and the TSC advises.** More democratic, but puts security-
  sensitive decisions with people who may not be qualified to make them.
- **Leaving it to the Founding Charter.** The Charter covers the whole organisation; the
  protocol's decisions need a policy implementers can find next to the specification.

## 8. Testing and migration

Nothing to test in code. On acceptance: `DECISIONS.md` is created with every decision so far;
GOVERNANCE.md links to this SNIP where it says thresholds are set elsewhere; comutability.org gains
Council and Decisions pages; the first quarterly founder-phase report is published.
