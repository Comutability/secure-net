## 1. Problem

GOVERNANCE.md gives the Technical Steering Committee (TSC) the decision on every SNIP, and a
future Community Council the appeals. It deliberately leaves out how either body is formed,
how many members must agree, how long they serve, how they are held to account, and when the
founder-led start ends. GOVERNANCE.md says those are for the Founding Charter and its
supplementary decisions. Until they are written down:

- every decision rests on one person, the founding steward, without a stated end to that;
- nobody outside can tell how to join, object or appeal in practice;
- the protocol can't credibly claim to be independent of Comutability's own products.

## 2. Proposed change

### 2.1 How this policy is adopted and changed

- This SNIP goes through every stage and minimum period in SNIP-0009 2.3. It is then adopted as a
  **supplementary decision under the Founding Charter v1.1**, recorded in the decision register
  (2.8) by the authorised human decision-maker, as GOVERNANCE.md requires. The TSC's own
  decision on it is recorded as conflicted (2.3).
- GOVERNANCE.md's "Who decides" and "Accountability" sections are amended to point here.
- **Later changes** to this policy or to GOVERNANCE.md follow the same route. Once the Community
  Council exists, they also need its approval by two thirds of its members. Before then, any change
  that weakens the independence rules, lengthens the founder phase or narrows appeals needs a
  Public Review of at least 60 days.

### 2.2 Definitions

- **Person**: a human being. Accounts run by AI or other automation are not people. A
  contribution authored by an AI does not count as anyone's contribution for eligibility, even
  when a person submitted it. Each person counts once, however many accounts they use.
- **Independent**: a person is independent if, in the past 12 months, they have not been employed
  by, paid by, contracted to, or held office in Comutability, or any organisation that builds a
  product for it, and are not the founding steward. An implementation is independent if it is
  written and maintained mainly by independent people.
- **Contribution**: a change merged into the Secure Net repository (specification, code, tests,
  documentation or translation), or a published review on one of its pull requests, by a person.
- **Members entitled to vote**: a body's members, less any who have stepped aside for a conflict.

### 2.3 The Technical Steering Committee

**Who decides what**

| | Technical Steering Committee | Community Council |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decides | SNIPs; part statuses; releases of the standard | Appeals; approval of governance changes (2.1); confirming TSC appointments; recalls |
| Does not | Change governance alone | Write or rewrite specifications |
| Size | 3 to 5 | 7 |

**Seats.** Once it has three members: at least one is independent; no two members are employed or
paid by the same organisation.

**Security review.** For each security-sensitive SNIP, the TSC names its security reviewer, a
qualified person other than the author, before Technical Review starts, and records the name. The
reviewer is replaced only with written reasons. An objection from any security reviewer who
reviewed the SNIP stands until it is answered in writing.

**Appointment.**

- Anyone may nominate a person, including themselves.
- The nomination states whether the person is independent, and is open for public comment for 30
  days.
- The TSC then decides within 30 days of the comment period closing, with written reasons if it
  declines. If it hasn't decided by then, the nominee is appointed provisionally, as if accepted.
  A refusal can be appealed (2.6).
- The Community Council confirms each appointment once it exists, including every appointment
  made before it existed, at its first meeting.

**Terms.** Two years, renewable, staggered so that no more than half the seats end in the same
year. A member may step down at any time.

**Decisions.**

- *Quorum*: more than half of the members entitled to vote take part (yes, no or abstain).
- *Ordinary decisions* (additive changes, rejections, releases): more yes than no votes, and at
  least half of the members entitled to vote say yes.
- *Special decisions* (breaking changes, marking a part Stable, deprecating or withdrawing
  something, licence changes, and recommending governance changes): at least two thirds of members
  entitled to vote, rounded up, say yes. A security-sensitive change also needs no unresolved
  security objection.
- *Classification*: if any member or the security reviewer says a change is breaking, it is treated
  as breaking unless a special decision says otherwise.
- *Ties and failed votes*: the proposal stays at its stage. It may be put again after it changes,
  or after 30 days.

**Conflicts of interest.**

- A member with a material interest in a proposal says so in the record. Being its author counts.
- Outside the founder phase, a conflicted member steps aside. If that leaves no quorum, the
  decision waits. After 60 days, the Community Council (or, before it exists, the remaining members)
  may appoint a temporary independent member for that decision only.

**AI.** As GOVERNANCE.md says: AI may prepare proposals, code, tests and reviews, but can't be a
member, can't vote, can't stand, and its approval never stands in for a human's.

### 2.4 The founder phase

The founder phase lasts until the TSC has three members, including one independent member. If
the Community Council exists by then, it must also have confirmed them; otherwise it confirms them
at its first meeting (2.3), and the phase is treated as over in the meantime. The Council may form
before or after the founder phase ends. During it:

- **Who decides.** The founding steward is the provisional TSC. Every decision record says
  "decided by the single-member provisional TSC".
- **Conflicted decisions.** When the steward decides a proposal they wrote or have an interest in,
  the record says so. The decision also needs one of:
  - a written review by an independent person;
  - an extra 30 days of Public Review that ends with no unanswered objection.
- **Accepted.** A part may be Accepted only after a named, qualified security reviewer other than
  the steward has reviewed its security-sensitive content. Such parts are marked "Accepted
  (provisional)".
- **Stable.** No part may be marked Stable.
- **Provisional decisions after the founder phase.**
  - The first full TSC reconfirms or reopens each provisional decision.
  - Until it does, the decision stays "Accepted (provisional)". The quarterly report lists those
    still waiting.
  - Reopening never invalidates a wire format sites already use: any change goes through a SNIP
    and, where needed, deprecation (CVP-000 section 6).
- **Appeals.** Appeals go to the founding steward, who answers in writing within 14 days. If the
  appellant isn't satisfied and the Community Council exists, it decides the appeal as in 2.6. If
  the Council doesn't exist yet, the appellant may renew the appeal before it within six months of
  its first meeting.
- **Reports.** Every three months the steward publishes:
  - how many independent contributors there are;
  - open nominations;
  - provisional decisions;
  - what is being done to recruit members.
- **Time limit.** If the founder phase hasn't ended 18 months after this policy is adopted, the
  steward publishes why, and a SNIP reviewing this policy opens with a 60-day Public Review.

If the TSC later falls below three members, or loses its independent member, the Accepted and
Stable rules above apply again until it doesn't. Decisions already made stand.

### 2.5 The Community Council

**Code of conduct.** Comutability adopts a code of conduct before the first election.

**When it forms.** The first election starts within three months of whichever comes first:

- 15 independent people have made contributions in the past 12 months, counted from a list the
  steward publishes quarterly. Anyone may challenge an entry or an omission, and challenges are
  answered publicly.
- Two independent implementations have published conformance statements.
- 24 months have passed since this policy was adopted.

**Seats: seven.**

- Four are elected by contributors; at most one of them may be held by someone who isn't
  independent.
- Two are elected by implementers.
- One is held by Michael J Burgess as founding steward until the second election. It then becomes
  a contributors' seat.

**Electorate.**

- Contributors: every person with a contribution in the past 12 months.
- Implementers: one vote for each implementation with a published conformance statement, cast by
  its maintainers. All of Comutability's own implementations together share one vote.

**Candidates.** Any person eligible to vote in either electorate may stand, for one seat only.

**Method.**

- Secret ballot, using ranked voting (single transferable vote) for each group of seats.
- The protocol's own voting (CVP-012) on The Agora may be used to cast votes. Until CVP-012 is
  Accepted, the official count is the one published on GitHub.
- The count is run by two people, at least one of them independent and neither a candidate, and
  the anonymised tally is published.

**Terms.** Two years. At the first election, the candidates elected with the fewest votes in each
group serve one year, so that about half the seats come up each year.

### 2.6 Appeals

1. Anyone may appeal a TSC decision within 30 days, by opening an issue on the proposal that says
   what is wrong: the process wasn't followed, a comment went unanswered, or the decision causes a
   harm (security, privacy, accessibility) the TSC didn't weigh.
2. The TSC answers in writing within 14 days.
3. If the appellant isn't satisfied, the Community Council decides within 30 days. It may uphold
   the decision, or send it back to the TSC with written reasons. It can't rewrite the
   specification. Members who took part in the decision under appeal don't vote.
4. If the TSC, after a decision has been sent back twice, makes it again, the Council may overturn
   it by two thirds of its members entitled to vote.
5. An appeal doesn't suspend a decision unless the TSC or Council says so. A part under appeal is
   not included in a new standard edition until the appeal is settled.
6. After the founder phase but before the Council exists, step 3 is a review by an independent
   person the appellant and the TSC agree on. If they can't agree within 14 days, the appellant may
   renew the appeal before the Council within six months of its first meeting.

### 2.7 Recalls

- **TSC members**: a recall is proposed by ten contributors or by a third of the other members. The
  Community Council decides it (before it exists, the other TSC members), by two thirds.
- **Council members**: a recall is proposed by a tenth of the electorate that elected them (at least
  ten people), or by two thirds of the other Council members. It is decided by a vote of that
  electorate.
- **Grounds**: persistent absence, an undeclared conflict, or conduct against the code of conduct.
- **Fairness**: the member is heard first and doesn't vote on their own recall.
- **The founding steward**: once the Council exists, their seats can be recalled like anyone
  else's. During the founder phase, the safeguards are those in 2.4.

### 2.8 Records

- Every decision is written into its SNIP's section 10, with who took part, how each member voted,
  declared conflicts and reasons.
- `DECISIONS.md` in this repository is the protocol's part of Comutability's decision register:
  the same register, not a second one. comutability.org shows it.
- Meetings are minuted publicly. A session is private only for an embargoed security issue or a
  personal matter, and its minutes then say what was discussed in general terms and when the rest
  will be published.

## 3. Benefits and intended uses

It turns "independent" in GOVERNANCE.md into something checkable. It gives implementers and
contributors a known route in, a known route to object, outside checks on the founder phase, and
dates by which it must end or be reviewed.

## 4. Security and privacy

Security-sensitive changes keep their mandatory human security review, which is strengthened
during the founder phase. TSC and Council votes are recorded by name. Elections are secret, and
only anonymised tallies are published.

## 5. Compatibility

None: no wire format, label or rule between implementations changes.

## 6. Effects on independent implementations

They gain at least one independent TSC member once the founder phase ends, two Council seats
chosen by implementers, a right of appeal, and a path to Stable that needs them. Comutability's
own applications propose and appeal like anyone else, and their implementations share a single
implementer vote.

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **One Standards Council** combining technical and community seats. Simpler, but mixes technical
  review with representation and leaves no separate appeal.
- **The Community Council decides and the TSC advises.** More democratic, but puts
  security-sensitive decisions with people who may not be qualified to make them.
- **Counting site operators as an electorate.** Rejected for now: a new site is cheap to set up, so
  one vote per site is easy to game.
- **Leaving it all to the Founding Charter.** The Charter covers the whole organisation; the
  protocol's decisions need a policy implementers can find next to the specification.

## 8. Testing and migration

Nothing to test in code. On adoption:

- `DECISIONS.md` is created with every decision so far;
- GOVERNANCE.md is amended as in 2.1;
- comutability.org gains Council and Decisions pages;
- the first quarterly founder-phase report and contributor list are published.
